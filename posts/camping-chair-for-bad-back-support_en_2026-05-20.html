What Actually Matters When Choosing a Camping Chair for Bad Back Support

  • Lumbar support that's structural, not decorative. A removable foam pillow strapped to a sling chair does almost nothing after 30 minutes. What you actually want is a frame-integrated lumbar curve — like STRONGBACK's patented design — that physically prevents your lower spine from collapsing inward. If the product description says "lumbar pillow included," that's not the same thing.
  • Back height matters as much as back support. A 26" high back covers your shoulders, not just your lower spine. Shoulder tension and upper back pain are often downstream effects of poor lumbar alignment, so full-back coverage stops the chain reaction before it starts. Chairs under 20" of back height are almost always inadequate for anyone with chronic back issues.
  • Seat height affects how much your back works to stand up. The Portal's 18" seat height is intentional. Chairs that put you at 12"–14" off the ground force your lower back to do serious work every time you stand — which adds up over a weekend trip. If you have disc problems or hip flexor tightness, prioritize seat heights between 17" and 20".
  • Frame rigidity on uneven terrain. A chair that wobbles or flexes sideways on soft ground throws your pelvis out of alignment and undoes every bit of lumbar support the design provides. Look for alloy steel or reinforced steel frames with thick-walled tubing — not thin aluminum that bends under load.
  • Weight capacity relative to your size. Don't buy a chair rated for 250 lbs if you weigh 220 and plan to sit in it for hours. Frame flex under near-maximum load destroys support geometry. The Portal's 400 lb rating means most adults are sitting well within its structural sweet spot, which keeps the support consistent throughout the day.

What seat height should I look for if I have lower back or hip problems?

Aim for 17"–20" off the ground. At this height, your hips sit at or slightly above knee level, which reduces strain on your lumbar discs and hip flexors when you sit down or stand up. Chairs lower than 15" put your knees above your hips and increase spinal compression significantly — avoid them if back or hip pain is a concern.
